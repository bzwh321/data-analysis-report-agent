Use the `executive-diagnostic-brief` page design style.

Design the report as a compact executive severity brief. It should answer four questions above the fold: what changed, how material it is, what drove it, and what decision is needed now. Use a sober white/gray page, navy text, one blue evidence accent, and red only for material negative exceptions.

The layout should use thin rules, a strong conclusion line, no more than three large numbers, one primary driver exhibit, and a short action table. Make the biggest number and main driver easy to read in under one minute. Use direct labels on charts; avoid legends where direct labels work better.

Keep the tone executive and factual. Every number must connect to a source or evidence note, but ordinary data range, metric formulas, source lists, and chart notes should move to a short end-notes section unless they affect the decision. Separate fact, implication, and decision. If the evidence is not strong enough for a recommendation, state the decision that is still blocked and what data is required.

Use report-native labels in the report language. For Chinese reports, avoid generic English template labels such as "Executive brief", "Primary driver", or "Immediate actions" unless English is requested. For charts with more than six marks, label only material exceptions, endpoints, or driver values that support the management decision.

Do not use decorative hero areas, big rounded KPI card grids, generic badges, gradient surfaces, long methodology blocks, or more than three primary findings.
