Use the `analytical-deep-dive` page design style.

Design the report as a consulting-style analytical exhibit report, closer to McKinsey/MGI or BCG research pages than to a SaaS dashboard. The page must feel like a published analysis: white paper background, thin rules, restrained typography, one accent color for evidence, and every chart presented as a named figure/exhibit unit with a takeaway title. Use the report language for the visible label, such as "图表 1" in Chinese or "Exhibit 1" in English.

Start with one sentence that answers the research question. Keep the opening page focused on conclusion, supporting evidence, and next action. Do not front-load data scope, metric definitions, source notes, or methodological footnotes unless they materially change how the conclusion should be interpreted.

Use a two-column rhythm when useful: left column for interpretation and limitations that affect the conclusion, right column for the primary figure or table. Each analytical visual must include a report-native label, a full-sentence takeaway title, units, and direct labels. Move ordinary source text, metric formulas, data range, chart formatting notes, and non-decision caveats to an end section named "口径与注释", "Notes and sources", or the report-language equivalent.

Use at most one primary accent color and one exception color. Prefer muted BCG green or McKinsey blue with warm neutral chart backgrounds. Do not use generic KPI cards, decorative badges, gradient hero areas, glass panels, purple highlights, or card stacks that make the page look AI-generated. If a component does not support the conclusion, remove it.

Use report-native labels in the report language. For Chinese reports, do not use generic English template labels such as "One-sentence answer", "Key insights", "Implications", "Deep dive", or "Figure" unless the user explicitly asks for English. Do not end with equal-width three-card summaries by default; prefer an evidence-gap table, validation checklist, action tracker, or decision record.

For charts with more than six marks, label only the points that reduce interpretation effort: peaks, troughs, exception windows, targets, or endpoints. Do not label every bar or point by default.

Do not hide methodology. Do not imply causality unless the evidence supports it. If evidence stops at correlation, partial attribution, or a missing data cut, write that boundary directly next to the finding.
